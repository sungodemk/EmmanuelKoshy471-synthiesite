Organ Synthesizer

Owner: Emmanuel Koshy

A Hammond B3-style organ built from up to nine drawbar-controlled sine waves per note, with an attack/release envelope, shared vibrato, and a Leslie effect.

Organ demo

Organ only. Hear drawbars, two registrations, the envelope on short notes, then vibrato and Leslie on a held chord.

Tonewheels

Each note's CDrawbarWave holds nine sine generators, one per drawbar. Each keeps its phase in cycles and advances it each sample by frequency × multiple × sample period.

Drawbars

The note's drawbars attribute (for example, 88 8000 000) sets the nine standard drawbars and their frequency multiples:

Drawbar16'5⅓'8'4'2⅔'2'1⅗'1⅓'1'
Multiple1×3×2×4×6×8×10×12×16×

Setting 8 is full amplitude; each step down is −3 dB. For setting n from 1 to 8:

amplitude = 10^(−3 × (8 − n) / 20)

For example, settings 8, 7, and 5 give amplitudes 1.0, 0.708, and 0.354. Setting 0 turns a tonewheel off. Active sines are summed and normalized to avoid clipping.

Envelope generation

Each note passes through a CAR envelope: a 10 ms attack and a 50 ms release that prevents clicks. The release fits within the note duration to preserve timing. Duration is in beats and converted to seconds using the score's BPM.

Vibrato

A B3's vibrato affects all notes together. One oscillator in the synthesizer computes this factor each sample:

factor = 1 + vibratodepth × sin(2π × vibratorate × t)

The factor multiplies all nine tonewheel frequencies on every playing note. At vibratodepth="0.01", 1000 Hz varies by ±10 Hz and 3000 Hz by ±30 Hz. Set rate and depth on the <score> tag.

Leslie speaker

The shared CLeslie processes the combined organ output and models a rotating horn with two effects:

  • Amplitude modulation: louder toward the listener, quieter away.
  • Stereo rotation: opposite-side microphones make the sound sweep between speakers.

Set lesliespeed on the <score> tag: about 0.8 Hz for slow chorale or 6.5 Hz for fast tremolo. Doppler pitch shift was omitted because it added artifacts. This model uses one rotor rather than separate horn and bass rotors; headphones make the effect easiest to hear.
